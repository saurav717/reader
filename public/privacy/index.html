Privacy policy

What the paper reader at koios.dev/ — “the app” below — does with your data, and what it never sees. This page covers the app and nothing else on this site.

Last updated 18 October 2026.

The short version. The app runs in your browser. It has no database and no account of its own: your library — the papers you collect, your highlights and notes, your reading progress — lives in your own Google Drive, with a copy cached in your browser. The one server involved is a small proxy that fetches what a browser cannot fetch directly and, for people signed in, runs a few paid features on the site's own keys. Nothing it sees is sold, used for advertising, or used to train anything.

What the app stores, and where

In your Google Drive. When you sign in, your library is a folder in your own Drive, Papers_collection: a library.json with your collections, projects and highlights, and a folder per paper holding its PDF, a JSON file of its details and your highlights, and your notes. These are ordinary files in your Drive. You can read, move or delete them yourself at any time, and deleting them there needs nothing from the app.

In this browser. Settings, the page you were on, and preferences such as the calendar's view are kept in the browser's localStorage. A copy of your library is cached in the browser's IndexedDB, one per account, so the app opens quickly; Drive's copy is the one that counts, and signed in, the app opens on it. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes all of this and nothing else.

In a Git repository, only if you configure one. The app can mirror your notes and bibliography to a repository you name, and keep a project's code in one. See GitHub below for where the token goes.

On the proxy. The proxy keeps no copy of your library, your papers, your notes or your calendar. What it does keep is set out under The proxy.

What the app receives from Google

Signing in with Google is how you open your library, and it is asked for first. Each further permission is asked for only when you first use the feature that needs it, in a window of Google's that names the scope. None of them lets the app see anything beyond what is listed here.

The access token Google issues lasts about an hour and is held in the browser's session storage, beside whose it is. So that you need not sign in again every hour, the app also keeps a refresh token from the same consent: in this browser's localStorage on the site, and in an encrypted file on your computer in the Koios desktop app. When the hour is up the app sends that refresh token to the proxy, which exchanges it with Google for a new access token and keeps neither; the proxy never stores a refresh token, and nothing is renewed except while the app is open and in use on your account. Sign out gives the grant back to Google and deletes the refresh token where it was kept, and Forget beside the calendar in Settings drops that permission in the browser. You can withdraw any of these for good at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Limited Use. The app's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used only to provide the features described on this page. It is never sold, never used for advertising, never used to train any model, and never transferred to anyone except as needed to provide those features or as required by law.

The proxy

The hosted copy of the app is built with a proxy, a Cloudflare Worker run by me. The browser cannot fetch most PDFs or some search results directly, so those requests go through it. Signed in, your browser sends the proxy your Google sign-in once; the proxy asks Google whose it is and answers with a short-lived pass — your email address and an expiry, signed — which the app then sends with its requests. The proxy records, per account, how much of the site's own keys each person used: PDF fetches, searches, and the AI and web-search calls below. Those totals are visible only to me, on a page nobody else can open. The proxy keeps no copy of what was fetched.

What the app sends elsewhere

Searching and opening papers means talking to the places papers live. Your search terms and the identifiers of the papers you open are sent to whichever of these answers the request:

  • arXiv, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref and Unpaywall, for search results and metadata; Google Scholar, through the proxy, when you choose it;
  • Google (accounts.google.com, googleapis.com), for sign-in, Drive, Calendar and Colab;
  • Zoom (zoom.us), through the proxy, only when you ask it to make a meeting, and Granola (public-api.granola.ai), through the proxy with your own key, only when you have set one;
  • Recall.ai (recall.ai), only in the Koios app and only for a meeting you chose to record (or set to Koios notes): its recorder sends the meeting's sound to Recall.ai to be transcribed, the transcript comes back to the app, and the notes written from it are filed in your project. The proxy asks Recall.ai for each recording's upload token with the site's key and keeps nothing of the meeting;
  • Google Fonts, for the typefaces the page uses;
  • the proxy above, for PDFs and for everything marked “through the proxy” here;
  • a dictionary and Wikipedia, for the lookup card over a selection.

AI features. Ask AI, Explain and the lookup card's background paragraph send the text of the paper you are reading — and your question, and, where you ask it, pictures of the pages or figures on screen — to the model you pick: Anthropic's Claude, DeepSeek, or Google's Gemini, through the proxy on the site's keys or directly on a key you enter in Settings. Nothing is sent until you use one of these features. The Web button sends your question to a web search service through the proxy. Answers you keep go into your notes, in your Drive.

Writing a paper. The Write tab sends a draft's LaTeX to a compiler to make its PDF — the Companion on your own computer, a Cloud Run service run by me, or GitHub Actions in your repository — and to Overleaf, if you link an Overleaf project. The compiler keeps nothing once the PDF is returned.

GitHub. If you connect GitHub — with Koios's GitHub App, a sign-in, or a pasted token — the token goes to the proxy, which checks it with GitHub and keeps it sealed, for your account alone, with a key only the proxy holds. It is never sent back to any page and nothing of it stays in the browser. GitHub calls go through the proxy, which makes only the few the app needs: commits, a repository made, read or turned private. A token unused for six months is forgotten; one revoked on GitHub, at once.

Your own computer. The Koios Companion, if you install it, runs on your machine and talks only to your browser and to the services you point it at; nothing from it passes through the proxy.

Each of these services has its own privacy policy, and I have no control over what they log. Beyond these requests the app sends nothing anywhere. It carries no analytics, no advertising and no third-party trackers of any kind, and it sets no cookies.

Deleting your data

All of it, at once: Settings → Account → Delete my account. Before anything goes, it lists what is kept about you, then deletes:

  • On the proxy: your usage totals; the invitations you sent or were sent, and the site's mail to or from you; your connected computers; your GitHub connection (given back to GitHub) and your Overleaf accounts; and this browser's session in the browser pane, with the sign-ins it kept.
  • Shared projects: one you own is closed for everyone in it; from anyone else's you are removed, and what you wrote in its logbook stays with that project without your address.
  • In this browser: its copy of your library, your settings and the keys you saved; you are signed out and Google's access for the app is revoked.
  • In your Drive, only if you tick it: the app's files — Papers_collection, wherever you moved it — are moved to Drive's trash, which keeps them for 30 days, and the hidden app-data copies of library.json and playgrounds.json are deleted. Otherwise they stay yours, untouched.

The pass the proxy issues at sign-in is kept nowhere, so nothing of it remains. The mail provider that sent an invitation keeps its own delivery log for a time, under its own policy.

Piece by piece: delete the Papers_collection folder in Drive yourself; clear site data for koios.dev in your browser; revoke Google's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions; disconnect GitHub or Overleaf in Settings, which has the proxy forget the token. Or write to the address below.

What this is

The app searches public indexes of research papers and opens papers from the places that publish them; keeps what you collect in your Google Drive and shows your Google Calendar beside it; explains papers and answers questions about them with AI models; turns a paper into a project — code, data, experiments and a draft — that runs on Google Colab, on a server you link, or on your own computer through the optional Companion, with coding agents you run under your own accounts; compiles a paper for you; and lets you share a project with people you invite. A small proxy, run by me, fetches what a browser cannot, relays a few features on the site's own keys for the people signed in, and holds the little that collaboration and moving files between machines need.

Your computer, and the machines you run code on

Installing the Companion lets the app run code, terminals and agents on your computer as you, and, by default once an account claims it, opens a tunnel so that your other browsers and machines can reach it from the internet, guarded by its token. Linking a server, or running on Colab, lets the app run code there as you. Coding agents the app starts — Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and others — run under your own accounts with their providers, can read, change, create and delete files in the project they are given, and act on what they read. You decide what to pair, what to link, where to put a project's files, which agent to run and what to let it do, and you are responsible for what runs on your machines and accounts as a result. The privacy policy says exactly what each of these exposes and to whom.
